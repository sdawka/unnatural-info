# Act 1 — What moves us

> The first act starts with orientation: what a living system approaches, avoids, preserves, or changes. It follows wanting into difference, care, meaning, and the shared work of knowing.

## Overview

The act uses **will** as a functional word, not a verdict about consciousness. A bacterium can be organized around a chemical gradient without having a human inner life. A person can feel a pull and still question, redirect, or refuse it. The useful question is what the system is organized to do next.

Chapter 1 separates orientation, feeling, and reflective choice. Chapter 2 asks what happens when distinct histories and needs meet. Chapter 3 treats love, growth, and meaning as related but nonidentical: care can involve duty and limits; growth can be harmful; meaning can include rest, grief, and maintenance. Chapter 4 asks how particular viewpoints become more reliable shared knowledge through evidence, correction, communication, and attention to whose testimony is heard.

The act draws selectively on Damasio, Spinoza, embodied cognition, Buber, Murdoch, Wolf, Nagel, and Fricker. They disagree with one another. The archive records them as companions and challenges, not as a single proof.

## Open questions

- What explains orientation at all, and when does orientation become experience?
- Which desires should be endorsed, and by what ethical reasons?
- How can shared inquiry correct for power, exclusion, and testimony that is discounted?
