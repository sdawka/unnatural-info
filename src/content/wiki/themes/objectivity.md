# Objectivity

> Objectivity is disciplined approximation: evidence, correction, communication, and a willingness to hear reports that a group has discounted.

Chapter 4 begins with a practical problem. Each person sees from somewhere, but a table, staircase, deadline, or injury can resist what any one person believes. Shared knowledge grows when reports are compared, claims are recorded, and the world can answer back. This does not make consensus identical with truth. Groups can agree on falsehood, and an accurate witness can be ignored.

Objectivity therefore has an ethical as well as evidential side. Miranda Fricker's *Epistemic Injustice* helps name credibility deficits and gaps in shared concepts. Asking “who was heard?” belongs beside asking “what evidence supports this?”

Chapters 6, 11, and 24 extend the point. A map is useful for a purpose and incomplete outside it. Categories can hide people or distribute costs. A revisable model should state what it explains, what it leaves out, and what would change confidence.
