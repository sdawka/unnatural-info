# Wisdom traditions

Chapter 22 compares practices from different traditions only after describing their own aims and language. A Buddhist mindfulness practice, Epictetus's attention to judgment, Daoist non-forcing, or a Christian contemplative exercise may overlap in what a reader notices, while differing in metaphysics, ethics, community, and desired transformation.

MMM can be used as a secondary comparison: attention may resemble observing; a teaching may organize an account; conduct may return the account to life. That mapping is the book's interpretation. It does not show that the traditions were secretly doing science, that they converge on one structure, or that one practice is effective for everyone.

Becker offers another interpretation of mortality and religious meaning, but it is contested and incomplete. Traditions can address death, attachment, compassion, community, beauty, obligation, and liberation in different proportions. Preserve those differences before compressing them.

## Practice

The [attention practice](/practice/attention) asks readers to attend to a cup's use, texture, wear, and associations without requiring a mystical insight. Record what attention changed and what the exercise cannot establish.
