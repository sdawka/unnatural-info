# Meaning: coherence, purpose, significance

Psychological research often distinguishes three aspects of meaning in life: coherence (life makes enough sense to navigate), purpose (something can be aimed at), and significance (one's life matters). These are useful comparison terms, not proof that the book's account of meaning is identical to a validated psychological model.

Chapter 3 treats meaning as related to care and sustained engagement. A person may find coherence in an explanation, purpose in a commitment, and significance in a relationship or activity that matters. None guarantees well-being, and meaningfulness can coexist with grief, fatigue, obligation, or uncertainty. Susan Wolf's account is a useful challenge to reducing meaning to intense preference: worthwhile objects and active engagement matter too.

The framework's vocabulary can illuminate a problem, but the fit should remain revisable. A stalled plan may need a clearer account, a smaller action, better material conditions, rest, or another person's help. It is not automatically a failed “loop,” and visible impact is not the only source of significance.

## Questions

- What makes this situation intelligible enough to act in?
- What aim is actually yours, and what is imposed by fear or status?
- What matters here even if it produces no impressive trace?
