# Growth and learning

Growth in UNNATURAL means changed capacity, not a guaranteed climb toward superiority. A person may become better at a skill, better at noticing a limit, or better at asking for help. A group may learn a rule that protects access, or learn that a cherished plan causes harm. Rest, maintenance, grief, and choosing enough belong in the picture.

Chapters 13–18 call three overlapping modes MMM: Learning (observe, explain, test), Creating (find your bearings, try, make), and Becoming (attend, explore, make room). The name is a synthesis for readers, not evidence that cells, people, evolution, and institutions run one identical loop. Evolutionary population change, individual learning, and cultural transmission should be compared carefully.

Existing traditions help sharpen the comparison. Retrieval practice gives one concrete learning action; implementation intentions can turn a chosen cue into a small plan. Neither source proves the larger framework, and neither guarantees that a plan succeeds.

The same question about improvement reaches beyond an individual. Chapter 20 follows printing and the Internet: increased reach opens possibilities, while people still have to judge purposes, costs, and who can repair the arrangements. Chapter 21 develops symbolic culture as a way living beings preserve and share capacities across generations. Dependence on people and material support continues at every scale.

## Questions

- What capacity changed, and who benefits or pays?
- Did the change improve an outcome, or merely optimize a proxy?
- Is more effort needed, or is the right response rest, support, repair, or acceptance?
