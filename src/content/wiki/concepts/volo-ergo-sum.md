# Volo Ergo Sum

“I will, therefore I am” is the book's deliberately provisional reply to “what moves us?” It uses **will** as a functional lens for orientation: a system is organized around conditions it approaches, avoids, preserves, or changes. It does not claim that a bacterium has a human inner life, or that orientation settles consciousness.

Chapter 1 separates orientation, feeling, and reflective choice. Damasio's somatic-marker work supports a specific claim about bodily signals and decision-making. Spinoza offers a philosophical account of striving. Enactivist work treats cognition as something living bodies do in relation to environments. These sources illuminate different parts of the lens and do not form one proof.

Chapter 2 follows the practical consequence: distinct systems carry different histories and needs into shared situations. Chapter 3 asks how care, growth, and meaning relate without reducing one to another. Chapter 4 asks how private accounts become shared knowledge through evidence, correction, and testimony.

The phrase is a handle, not a throne. Try the [wants practice](/practice/wants), then record where the lens clarifies a choice and where it hides bodily need, social pressure, material constraint, or another person's claim.
