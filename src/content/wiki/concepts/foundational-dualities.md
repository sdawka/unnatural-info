# Foundational distinctions

Chapter 8 generalizes from the Three Blades to a practical question: what kind of distinction is this, and what does it leave between its sides? “Duality” is a convenient name, but not every useful division is binary. Some are gradients, overlapping categories, or temporary choices for a task.

Examples include self/world, toward/away, known/unknown, signal/noise, trust/threat, and being/becoming. The book does not claim they arrive in a fixed biological order or that a bacterium uses every human category. The point is to examine how a cut helps at one resolution and misleads at another.

The yin-yang tradition offers one alternative to treating opposites as enemies or expecting a final synthesis: poles can be mutually shaping without being identical. This is a comparison, not a claim that all dialectical traditions say the same thing.

## Practice

Choose a category in your life. Ask whether it is binary, graded, overlapping, or imposed by a form. Then name one case it handles badly. The [labels practice](/practice/labels) makes the omitted case visible.
