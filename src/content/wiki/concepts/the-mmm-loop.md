# MMM: Three overlapping modes

> MMM is a family nickname for ways of learning, creating, and becoming. It is a proposed synthesis, not a universal mechanism or a ladder of maturity.

## The three modes

### Learning: observe, explain, test

The Learning mode asks what can be known. You notice a difference, form an account of how relevant things may connect, and test the account against new observations or a deliberate comparison. A test can narrow possibilities without producing certainty. Learning need not deliver control: astronomy can teach us about stars without letting us move them.

### Creating: find your bearings, try, make

The Creating mode asks what can be done. You identify the situation and its constraints, make a move, and produce something that can meet the world. A draft, recipe, conversation, or prototype can teach before a complete explanation exists. Making can also cause harm, so effort and effectiveness are not ethical conclusions.

### Becoming: attend, explore, make room

The Becoming mode asks what deserves attention and what might emerge. You attend to what is present, explore possibilities without fixing the answer too early, and support conditions for an outcome without pretending to command it. Manifestation here means preparation and participation, never wishing a result into existence.

## How they relate

The modes can nest and interrupt one another. A cook tests a recipe while making dinner. A person planning an apology may need to stop gathering evidence and listen. Someone caring for a dying plant may need maintenance, acceptance, and grief rather than a more elaborate model. There is no fixed order and no claim that “Becoming” is a higher stage than Learning.

Use [One sheet, three kinds of doing](/practice/modes) to test an explanation, make a small object, and follow an unplanned shape with the same material. Notice how the purpose and the meaning of a result change. Rest, care, limits, material support, and asking for help remain available responses outside this comparison.

## What the concept does not establish

Three words recurring across fields do not prove that those fields share one process. Evolutionary change in a population is not an individual's learning. A contemplative practice has its own history and aims before this framework compares it. Similarity can suggest a fruitful analogy; it cannot serve as independent confirmation.
