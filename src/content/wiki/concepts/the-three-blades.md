# The Three Blades

The “blades” are memorable names for three useful distinctions in Act 2. They are lenses for handling situations, not discovered biological stages or universal opposites.

## First: self and world

A body, task, or relationship has a boundary that helps orient action. The boundary can leak, shift, and depend on context. Ask what this distinction helps protect or coordinate, and where it fails.

## Second: world and model

A description, expectation, or route is made from the world and used within it, but it is not the world. A model can guide action without being a full internal picture. The test is whether it helps with the task and what its omissions cost.

## Third: good and bad

Living systems distinguish conditions that support or damage them. Human values add history, reasons, institutions, and moral disagreement. A preference is not yet an ethical verdict.

Every cut makes something easier to handle and leaves something out. Use the [maps practice](/practice/maps) or [labels practice](/practice/labels) to name an omitted detail and who bears its consequence.
